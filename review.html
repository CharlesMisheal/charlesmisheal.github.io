Spiritual Portfolio Review

A review of every part of my portfolio for cohesion, with findings, the enhancements they lead to, and a plan to finalize the portfolio so it shows my professional and spiritual growth as one story.

Purpose and method

Elder Bednar taught that "the power of the Savior's gospel to transform and bless us flows from discerning and applying the interrelatedness of its doctrine, principles, and practices" (Bednar, 2018). I wanted to review my portfolio the same way, looking at how the parts connect, not only at whether each part is complete.

I read every page of the site from start to finish: Home, Résumé, Projects, Guiding Principles, Faith and Competencies, Reflections, Mentorship and Growth, the refined Metasploit report, and the repository README. For each one, I asked four questions:

  1. Does it point back to my mission, "Using technology to serve the one," and to my three guiding principles?
  2. Does it connect professional work and spiritual growth, or keep them in separate boxes?
  3. Is it accurate, current, and consistent with the other pages?
  4. Is it easy to find, read, and use on a phone, with a keyboard, and with a screen reader?

I also checked the navigation and links on every page and viewed each page at phone and desktop widths. The findings below are numbered (F1, F2, and so on) so each suggested enhancement can be traced back to what I found.

What is working well

  • A clear, shared mission. "Using technology to serve the one" (Luke 15:4–7) appears on the Home page and is echoed in the Bravick project, the principles, and the competencies analysis.
  • Three principles used consistently. Integrating Faith and Work, Ministering to the One, and Consistency in Small and Simple Things appear by name on the Guiding Principles, Faith and Competencies, and Projects pages.
  • Projects that include ethics, not only results. All four projects include an ethical considerations section, and the refined Metasploit report documents its decision-making and what it does not claim.
  • Varied, cited spiritual materials. The materials list includes scriptures, general conference addresses, Church videos, BYU–Idaho leadership readings, and personal experience, numbered from 1 to 14 across Weeks 3 and 4, with each item linked to the principle it supports.
  • Honest reflections. The reflections name real experiences, such as my plan to study medicine ending and teaching myself technology, rather than general statements.
  • A consistent, accessible design. Every page shares one stylesheet, a skip link, a labeled navigation with the current page marked, visible focus outlines, 44-pixel touch targets, and tables that scroll on small screens.

Cohesion findings

  1. F1. The portfolio explained my principles but not how I keep them. Each page shows faith and work connected, but nothing described what happens when they drift apart, or how I will stay aligned after the course ends. Without that, the portfolio reads as a finished picture instead of an ongoing practice.
  2. F2. The spiritual portfolio had no "map" for readers. The Reflections page lists entries, but a visitor cannot see how the principles, materials, competencies, reflections, and goals build on each other. Two of the three entries also point to whole pages rather than journal posts.
  3. F3. Faith is sometimes treated like a checklist item in the project narratives. Elder Bednar warned that "pharisaical focus upon checklists can divert us from drawing closer to the Lord" (Bednar, 2018). My projects show integration in their design and ethics, but the faith connection is often saved for the last paragraph, and three of the four notes do not name the principle they reflect.
  4. F4. Small inconsistencies on the Guiding Principles page. The page description, the spelling of CloudCity, and the lack of a link to newer work make it look older than it is.
  5. F5. The README no longer matched the site. Anyone who finds my portfolio through GitHub would get an incomplete picture.
  6. F6. A key part of my spiritual story is missing. My missionary service and Church membership are central to why my principles exist, but they do not appear on the Home page or résumé.
  7. F7. Goals without progress are hard to evaluate. The Mentorship and Growth page sets clear targets, but readers cannot see whether I am meeting them.
  8. F8. Menu length on small screens. Nine links take up a large part of a phone screen before the content begins.
  9. F9. Related pages were not linked to each other. The competencies table and the principles page refer to the same ideas without linking, so readers have to find the connection themselves.
  10. F10. The portfolio has not been tested by other people. I have reviewed it myself, but I have not yet watched someone else use it on a phone.

Design updates completed this week

  • Added the Integration Reflection and Spiritual Portfolio Review pages to the menu on every page, in the same order.
  • Made the menu more compact on phones without shrinking its touch targets below 44 pixels.
  • Added the portfolio overview and Week 5 entries to the Reflections page.
  • Linked principle names in the Faith and Competencies table to the Guiding Principles page.
  • Corrected the CloudCity spelling and the Guiding Principles page description.
  • Added a progress line to SMART goal 2 on the Mentorship and Growth page.
  • Updated the README page list and removed a stray space in the résumé menu code.

Finalization plan

The Savior taught, "In your patience possess ye your souls" (Luke 21:19). I will finish the portfolio in small, steady steps rather than all at once.

  1. This week (Week 5): publish the Integration Reflection and this review, and complete the design updates above (E1, E2, E4, E5, E8, E9).
  2. Before the final portfolio submission: add my missionary service to the Home page and résumé (E6), revise the "Faith in Practice" notes (E3), update the SMART goal progress (E7), and run the usability test (E11).
  3. Final check: read every page once more against the four review questions, check every link, and view each page on a phone.
  4. After the course and through graduation in August 2027: keep adding dated reflections every two weeks (E10), and review my mind map each block so the portfolio grows with me.

What this review taught me

President Kusch taught that a person with understanding "not only says what they know, but they act upon what they know" (Kusch & Kusch, 2024). Reviewing my portfolio showed me where I had described integration well and where I still needed to act on it. The strongest parts of my portfolio are the ones where faith changed a decision, like designing Bravick for schools that most software overlooks. The weakest parts are the ones where faith was added at the end. Finalizing this portfolio is a chance to practice the same principle I want to live in my career: let Christ shape the whole design, not just the last paragraph.
