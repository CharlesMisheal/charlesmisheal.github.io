Faith and Professional Competencies

How the skills I use at work connect to my three guiding principles and to what I believe.

I do not keep my faith in one box and my career in another. Elder Bednar taught that the Lord gathers "together in one all things in Christ." Below, I map five professional competencies to my guiding principles, with examples from my own work.

1. Technical problem-solving

Principle: Integrating Faith and Work

Problem-solving is where my agency becomes visible. Doctrine and Covenants 58:26–28 teaches that a wise servant does not wait to be "compelled in all things." When I saw schools that could not rely on the internet on exam day, nobody asked me to fix it. I chose to build Bravick, an offline-first platform that runs on one school computer and serves students over Wi‑Fi. On the retrieval pipeline, our team followed a disciplined path: define the problem, set honest baselines, test better methods, and measure each step. That matches the five stages in the BYU–Idaho leadership reading: define, generate, evaluate, pick, and plan. For me, solving problems well is part of being "anxiously engaged in a good cause."

2. Integrity and a security mindset

Principle: Integrating Faith and Work

Security skills can protect people or harm them, so they demand integrity. When I refined my IT 312 Metasploit report, I added a rules of engagement section, redacted password hashes even though the data came from a practice server, and wrote a section on ethical hacking and responsible disclosure. In Bravick, records stay on the school's own computer, separate from the program, with backups. I see data as a stewardship. The people whose information sits in a system trust me to guard it, whether or not anyone is checking.

3. Evaluation and honesty in AI

Principle: Ministering to the One

My benchmark found that the same AI models passed 96 to 103 of 105 cases with a careful prompt but only 9 to 57 with a plain one. Behind each failed case is a real person, perhaps a farmer or a student, who could receive a confident answer the evidence does not support. Evaluation is how I minister to that one person I will never meet. President Uchtdorf described belief as requiring "a little scientific curiosity." I try to bring that same curiosity and humility to testing: report what the data shows, including results that are not flattering.

4. Teaching and communication

Principle: Ministering to the One

Over more than five years, I have mentored over 300 learners in Python, web development, data science, and machine learning through CloudCity, using curricula I design. Good teaching starts where the learner is, not where I am. The same skill shows up in my technical writing. In my refined Metasploit report, I added a one-page summary in plain language so a manager could act on it. Clear communication is a form of respect. It means the person in front of me, or reading my work, can understand and act.

5. Collaboration and leadership

Principle: Consistency in Small and Simple Things

As team lead in TRI AI Saturdays Cohort 10, I learned that a team improves through small, steady habits: checking in, explaining an idea one more time, and keeping everyone moving together. The BYU–Idaho reading notes that groups struggle when members are at different stages, and that a leader keeps everyone "on the same stage and progressing together." In the HNG Internship, I worked in a team as a QA bug hunter, where careful, repeatable bug reports helped others fix problems. King Benjamin taught that service to others is service to God (Mosiah 2:17). Leadership, for me, is service done consistently.

What this analysis taught me

Mapping my competencies this way showed me that my strongest professional habits grew from spiritual ones. Daily scripture study taught me consistency, which made long debugging and evaluation work possible. Ministering taught me to ask who a system serves, which shapes how I design and test. Integrity taught me to report honestly, even when results are uncomfortable. My goal is to keep these strands woven together as I move into AI evaluation and research.
